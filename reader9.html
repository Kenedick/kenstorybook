BEFORE THE WORLD NOTICED HIM Episode 1: The Boy Nobody Saw Nobody noticed Ethan Reyes. Not his classmates. Not his teachers. Not even the people who sat beside him every day. To them, he was just an ordinary college student who always sat at the back of the classroom, wore simple clothes, and rarely spoke unless someone asked him a question. He wasn't popular. He wasn't rich. And he certainly wasn't the kind of person people remembered. But Ethan had a secret. He was talented at something the whole world would one day know his name for. He just hadn't found the courage to show it yet. --- ### The Invisible Student "Ethan, can you move? You're blocking the way." Ethan immediately stepped aside. "Sorry." The group of students walked past him without another glance. He adjusted the strap of his old backpack and continued toward his classroom. Another ordinary morning. Another day of being invisible. When he entered the room, his best friend, Lucas, waved at him from the back. "Bro! Over here!" Ethan sat beside him. "Did you finish the project?" Lucas asked. "Yeah. I sent you the file last night." Lucas opened his laptop and stared at the screen. "Wait. YOU made this?" Ethan nodded. "That's insane! This looks better than the examples our professor showed us!" "Don't exaggerate." "I'm serious! Why don't you join the university competition?" Ethan shook his head. "People like me don't win competitions." Lucas frowned. "People like you? What does that even mean?" Ethan didn't answer. Because he knew exactly what he meant. He had spent years watching other people get opportunities simply because they were confident, popular, or connected to the right people. Meanwhile, he had learned to stay quiet. It was easier that way. --- ### The Announcement That afternoon, their professor entered the classroom carrying a folder. "Everyone, I have an announcement." The students stopped talking. "Our university will be hosting a national innovation competition. The winning team will receive a scholarship, funding for their project, and an opportunity to present their work to industry professionals." The classroom immediately filled with excitement. "This is your chance to show what you can do," the professor continued. "Anyone interested should submit a proposal by Friday." Lucas turned toward Ethan with a huge grin. "This is perfect for you!" Ethan shook his head. "No way." "Why not?" "I'm not good enough." Lucas stared at him. "You're literally the smartest person I know when it comes to technology." "That doesn't mean I'll win." "No, but it means you should try." Ethan looked down at his notebook. He wanted to believe his friend. For years, he had been working on a project in secret—a simple idea that he hoped could eventually help students who struggled to access educational resources. He had never shown it to anyone. Not because it wasn't good. But because he was afraid people would laugh at him. --- ### The Girl Who Noticed "Ethan Reyes?" He looked up. A girl stood beside his desk, holding a stack of papers. She was Mia Anderson, one of the most well-known students in their department. She was confident, intelligent, and always surrounded by friends. "Yes?" he answered. "You're the one who created that program for our class last semester, right?" Ethan blinked. "You remember that?" "Of course. It helped everyone organize their assignments." He didn't know what to say. Mia smiled. "I heard about the innovation competition. Are you joining?" "No." "Why?" "Because I'm not interested." She tilted her head. "Really? Or are you just afraid of failing?" Ethan looked at her, surprised. She placed a paper on his desk. It was the competition application form. "You don't have to be famous to be talented, Ethan. Sometimes people just need a chance to see what you can do." Before he could respond, she walked away. Ethan stared at the application. For the first time, someone had spoken to him as if his work mattered. And strangely, that scared him more than being ignored. --- ### The First Step That night, Ethan sat at his desk, staring at his unfinished project. His laptop was old. His room was small. His resources were limited. But his idea was still there. Waiting. He opened a new document and began writing his proposal. For hours, he worked without stopping. He corrected errors, improved the design, and wrote down every detail he could think of. When he finally finished, the clock showed 2:17 a.m. He leaned back in his chair. His heart was pounding. He had done it. He had finally decided to give himself a chance. The next morning, Ethan arrived at the university before anyone else. He walked straight to the competition office and submitted his application. "Name?" the staff member asked. "Ethan Reyes." She typed something into her computer. "All right. Your application has been received." Ethan nodded and walked away. He didn't expect applause. He didn't expect anyone to congratulate him. He simply wanted to try. But what Ethan didn't know was that someone had been watching him from the hallway. A man in a dark suit held a folder containing Ethan's project details. He looked at the document and quietly said, "So, the boy finally decided to step forward." He took out his phone. "Yes," he said to the person on the other end. "I found him." A pause. "No, he doesn't know who he really is yet." The man glanced toward Ethan, who was walking across the campus without a clue about what was coming. "Things are about to change." To be continued.
